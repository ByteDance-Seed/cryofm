## Overview

Cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) is a powerful technique in structural biology and drug discovery, enabling the study of biomolecules at high resolution. 
Significant advancements by structural biologists using cryo-EM have led to the production of around 50k protein density maps at various resolutions on EMDB.[^emdb-count] 
However, cryo-EM data processing algorithms have yet to fully benefit from our knowledge of biomolecular density maps, with only a few recent models being data-driven but limited to specific tasks. 
In this study, we present cryoFM, a foundation model designed as a generative model, learning the distribution of high-quality density maps and generalizing effectively to downstream tasks. 
Built on flow matching, cryoFM is trained to accurately capture the prior distribution of biomolecular density maps. Furthermore, we introduce a flow posterior sampling method that leverages cryoFM 
as a flexible prior for several downstream tasks in cryo-EM and cryo-electron tomography (cryo-ET) without the need for fine-tuning, achieving state-of-the-art performance on most tasks and demonstrating 
its potential as a foundational model for broader applications in these fields.

In the training stage, cryoFM learns a vector field $\mathbf{v}_t(\mathbf{x}_t)$, whose corresponding probability flow generates the data
distribution $p_0(\mathbf{x}_0)$ of high-quality protein densities. In the inference stage, given a degraded observation $\mathbf{y}$, a likelihood
term $p_t(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{x}_t)$ is incorporated to convert the unconditional vector field $\mathbf{v}_t(\mathbf{x}_t)$ to a conditional one
$\mathbf{v}_t(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{y})$, so that we can sample from the posterior distribution $p_0(\mathbf{x}_0 \mid \mathbf{y})$. This enables
signal restoration of the density map, resulting in improved resolution of the alpha helices in the shown case.

**Key Contributions**
- **Density-space foundation model:** learns a generalizable prior over macromolecular density distributions.
- **Bayesian, controllable inference:** enables posterior sampling by combining the learned prior with a likelihood function defined by physics-based forward operators.


## Method at a Glance
### Flow matching prior
CryoFM employs flow matching to learn a generative prior over biomolecular density maps, enabling flexible posterior sampling for diverse downstream tasks without fine-tuning. Specifically:
- **Data:** 3D density patches (e.g., $64^3$), unsupervised pre-training on curated EMDB deposited maps
- **Model:** 3D HDiT[^hdit] predicting velocity for ODE-based generation

The following picture shows cryoFM's architecture. The side length $D$ of the input $\mathbf{x}_t$ undergoes dimension reduction through down-sampling 
layers and is then expanded back to its original size. To minimize computational cost near the input and output, 
the model employs neighborhood attention (NA). Neighborhood attention only attend to a localized area, whereas global attention (GA) 
calculates attention across all positions.

<OptimizedImage src="/assets/blog/cryofm1/hdit3d_arch.jpg" class="img-large" alt="hdit3d_arch" formats={["webp", "avif", "jpeg"]} loading="lazy" />
<small class="block article-caption">

CryoFM adopts the network architecture proposed in the HDiT paper[^hdit]. The attention mechanism in CryoFM employs two complementary strategies: neighborhood attention (NA) and global attention (GA). 
NA focuses on local spatial relationships within a constrained region, reducing computational complexity while maintaining fine-grained local features. 
In contrast, GA captures long-range dependencies across the entire volume, enabling the model to integrate global structural information. 
The following figure illustrates the QKV (Query, Key, Value) structure of both attention mechanisms, highlighting their distinct computational patterns.

### Flow Posterior Sampling

Given a degraded observation $\mathbf{y}$, we recover the clean signal $\mathbf{x}_0$ by combining the learned prior with the likelihood function through the following algorithm:

<div class="algorithm-block">
  <div class="algorithm-title">Algorithm 1: Flow Posterior Sampling</div>
  
  <div class="algorithm-require">
    <div class="algorithm-require-title">Require:</div>
    <div class="algorithm-require-item">$v_\Theta$: pretrained vector field $[0, 1] \times \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$</div>
    <div class="algorithm-require-item">$A$: forward operator $\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$</div>
    <div class="algorithm-require-item">$\mathbf{y}$: observation $\in \mathbb{R}^m$</div>
    <div class="algorithm-require-item">$N$: number of steps</div>
    <div class="algorithm-require-item">$\lambda_{\max}$: maximum step size</div>
  </div>

  <div class="algorithm-ensure">
    <div class="algorithm-ensure-title">Ensure:</div>
    <div class="algorithm-ensure-item">$\mathbf{x}_0$: recovered signal</div>
  </div>

  <div class="algorithm-steps">
    <div class="algorithm-step">
      <div class="algorithm-step-number">1:</div>
      <div class="algorithm-step-content">$\Delta t \leftarrow 1 / N$</div>
    </div>
    <div class="algorithm-step">
      <div class="algorithm-step-number">2:</div>
      <div class="algorithm-step-content">$\mathbf{x}_1 \leftarrow \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ <span class="algorithm-step-comment">// Initialize from standard normal</span></div>
    </div>
    <div class="algorithm-step">
      <div class="algorithm-step-number">3:</div>
      <div class="algorithm-step-content"><strong>for</strong> $t \in [1, 1-\Delta t, 1-2\Delta t, \ldots, \Delta t]$ <strong>do</strong></div>
    </div>
    <div class="algorithm-step">
      <div class="algorithm-step-number">4:</div>
      <div class="algorithm-step-content algorithm-step-content-indented">$\mathbf{x}'_{t-\Delta t} \leftarrow \mathbf{x}_t - \Delta t \cdot v_\Theta(t, \mathbf{x}_t)$</div>
    </div>
    <div class="algorithm-step">
      <div class="algorithm-step-number">5:</div>
      <div class="algorithm-step-content algorithm-step-content-indented">$l(\mathbf{x}_t) \leftarrow \|\mathbf{y} - A \hat{\mathbf{x}}_0(\mathbf{x}_t)\|^2$ <span class="algorithm-step-comment">// Likelihood loss</span></div>
    </div>
    <div class="algorithm-step">
      <div class="algorithm-step-number">6:</div>
      <div class="algorithm-step-content algorithm-step-content-indented">$\mathbf{g} \leftarrow \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} l(\mathbf{x}_t) / \|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_t} l(\mathbf{x}_t)\|_2$ <span class="algorithm-step-comment">// Normalize gradient for numerical stability</span></div>
    </div>
    <div class="algorithm-step">
      <div class="algorithm-step-number">7:</div>
      <div class="algorithm-step-content algorithm-step-content-indented">$\lambda'_t \leftarrow \min\{\lambda_{\max}, t / (1 - t)\}$ <span class="algorithm-step-comment">// Prevent the weighting term from being too large</span></div>
    </div>
    <div class="algorithm-step">
      <div class="algorithm-step-number">8:</div>
      <div class="algorithm-step-content algorithm-step-content-indented">$\mathbf{x}_{t-\Delta t} \leftarrow \mathbf{x}'_{t-\Delta t} - \lambda'_t \mathbf{g}$</div>
    </div>
    <div class="algorithm-step">
      <div class="algorithm-step-number">9:</div>
      <div class="algorithm-step-content"><strong>end for</strong></div>
    </div>
    <div class="algorithm-step">
      <div class="algorithm-step-number">10:</div>
      <div class="algorithm-step-content"><strong>return</strong> $\mathbf{x}_0$</div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

The algorithm iteratively refines the signal by:
1. Following the learned prior through the vector field $v_Θ$
2. Incorporating the likelihood constraint via gradient guidance
3. Using adaptive step sizes to ensure numerical stability

We demonstrate the effectiveness of our method on 4 synthetic tasks: Spectral noise de-noising, Anisotropic noise de-noising, Missing wedge in-painting, *Ab initio* modeling.
